## 1. GAME OVERVIEW

### Elevator Pitch
A grind-forward turn-based RPG set in a post-victory world where the heroes already won, death isn't permanent, and fighting is just a way of life. Collect Legends from shattered realities, rebuild your lost power from scratch, and push through an infinite loop of nodes, loot, and escalating strength. The grind isn't a means to an end — it IS the game.

### Core Fantasy
You already won once. You got soft. Now start over and become something even greater.

### Tone
Completely unhinged and self-aware. Robots cast fireballs. Ninjas fight soldiers. Henry Stickmin exists. Death is a minor inconvenience. Nobody questions any of it. The game knows what it is and leans in.

### World Theme — Fighting as a Way of Life
The BBeg is dead. The kingdom is thriving. And death isn't permanent — anyone who dies respawns at the location they fell within 3 to 7 days. This has completely changed society's relationship with combat. Fighting isn't feared — it's a passion, a craft, a lifestyle. Like extreme sports but the extreme sport IS combat and everyone is completely fine with this. Bandits aren't murderers, they're just people who chose a rougher hobby. Battles are how people socialize, settle disputes, test themselves, and have fun. The world APPROVES of fighting as a way of life.

### The Level Loss Problem
The original four defeated the BBeg years ago and were rewarded with everything they wanted. In the years since, with no battles to fight, their skills atrophied and their levels faded — use it or lose it. Specifically:
- **Fighter** — spent years eating spicy meat, tending his favorite vegetables, and enjoying endless Elven and Human concubines. The craft of combat faded completely.
- **Thief** — was deactivated and used as a vacuum cleaner. His combat routines literally weren't running.
- **Mage** — was in his tower vibe casting. Kept SOME magical ability but the battle-tested edge is gone.
- **Cleric** — probably maintained the most. Healing never fully stops being useful.

They're starting over. Not because they have to. Because they WANT to.

### Inspirations
- Combat feel: Persona 5
- Node progression: Warframe
- Loot system: Borderlands
- Roster depth: Disgaea
- Color/element system: Magic: The Gathering (loosely)
- Power ceiling: None

## 2. STORY & WORLD

### Setting
Fighter's Kingdom and the Shattered Realms — what remains of countless realities torn apart by a cosmic-level villain (the BBeg). The original four already won. The kingdom is peaceful and prosperous. But the shattering that happened during the conflict left cracks in reality that are still there, still growing, and still spilling the remnants of other worlds into this one.

### Death & Respawning
Death is not permanent in this world. Anyone who dies respawns at the location they fell within 3 to 7 days. This is known, accepted, and has shaped an entire culture around combat as a consequence-free way of life. Nobody is truly afraid of dying — it's an inconvenience, not a tragedy. This is why the entire world approves of fighting.

### The Shattering
An entity of immense power (the BBeg, details TBD) shattered multiple realities to harvest their power, legends, or something worse. The avatars of each realm — their greatest heroes — were scattered across the wreckage. The original four stopped the first BBeg years ago. But the shattering already happened and the cracks remain. Something new may be stirring in the aftermath.

### Realm Progression
- Early Game: Fighter's home realm — classic fantasy kingdom, the starting zone
- Early Game (post-Mage): The Shattering event occurs — Henry Stickmin faceplants next to the party and joins
- Mid Game: Heaven and Hell realms open (separate zones, separate nodes, everyone wants to fight)
- Mid Game (after Heaven and Hell): RGB Realm and BW (Black and White) Realm unlock
- Late Game: Push toward the source of the shattering, face the true threat
- Endgame: Infinite node scaling, uncapped grind, new realms TBD

### BBeg Invasion — Wave Structure
The BBeg's attack hits every realm simultaneously in four distinct waves. Each wave escalates the threat before the Echo arrives.

**Wave 1 — Eternal Night**
All realms simultaneously. The sun stops. Stars freeze. Purple bruising at the sky's edges. Passive and wrong. Nobody knows what it is yet.

**Wave 2 — The Crack**
A massive fracture tears open in the sky of every realm. Through it — visible, undeniable — Fighter's home realm. The legends can see another world through the crack. Nothing comes through. It's a window. The BBeg isn't hiding anymore. They WANT to be seen. Fighter's side sees the same cracks showing glimpses of other worlds — he doesn't know what he's looking at yet.

**Wave 3 — Enhanced Enemies**
Existing enemies in every realm get supercharged. Corrupted. Stronger, faster, wrong. Passive mobs turn aggressive then start attacking each other. Travel becomes dangerous. The legends are pinned down and worn down before the Echo even arrives.

**Wave 4 — Echo Tau Arrives**
The Echo doesn't announce itself. It's just there. The real fight begins.

---

### Echo Tau
Working name for the BBeg's agent sent to neutralize legends. Not a beam — an active hunter. It adapts, repositions, and KNOWS what the legends are trying to do. It watched before it struck. When Dire tries to reach his crafting bench, Echo Tau cuts him off — because it already knows what's on that bench.

**Visual design:** Completely breaks the visual language of whatever realm it enters. No blocky textures in the Minecraft realm, no pixel art rules. Smooth, alien, wrong proportions, unsettling colors. It looks like something from a different art style entirely. The visual clash IS the horror.

**Fighter and the main cast** will look equally out of place when they eventually enter other realms — visually foreign just like Echo Tau was. Intentional parallel.

### Realm Unlock Cutscenes — Design Rules
*These are PROPOSED intros for characters — subject to revision as development progresses.*

Every realm unlock opens with a cutscene showing that realm's legends across a four wave escalation — building up through gear tiers, not going straight to best equipment. The legends are genuinely threatening to Echo Tau. They make it WORK. They still lose.

**Cutscene structure per realm:**
1. Normal day — mundane opening, character doing their thing
2. Wave 1 hits — Eternal Night, passive reaction
3. Wave 2 — The Crack appears, acknowledgement
4. Wave 3 — Enhanced enemies, gear escalation begins
5. Wave 4 — Echo Tau arrives, full fight, final gear reached mid-battle
6. The Fall — earned, not cheap

## 3. STORY — ACT 1 OUTLINE

### The Setup
Fighter, the greatest hero of his realm, defeated the BBeg and was given a legendary retirement. A lush estate. A life of comfort. Gorgeous women. Everything a hero could want.

He was bored out of his mind.

### Beat 1 — The Escape
Fighter ignores his concubines, walks out the front door, and is immediately ambushed by bandits too stupid to recognize who he is. He gets captured and thrown in jail. He is not particularly worried about this.

### Beat 2 — Thief
While exploring the bandit hideout, Fighter finds a closet. Inside is a deactivated robot — Thief, his old party member, purchased from a pawn broker by the bandit leader who believed he was buying a vacuum cleaner. Fighter turns him on. Brief reunion.

### Beat 3 — The Bandit Leader's Sweetheart
The reunion is cut short when the bandit leader's sweetheart shows up. First real boss fight. She survives and limps away — establishing her as a recurring antagonist with a personal grudge against Fighter.

### Beat 4 — Node Clearing
Fighter and Thief push through a handful of nodes. The world opens up. The grind begins.

### Beat 5 — Cleric
They encounter Cleric mid-combat. She's already dealing with the bandit leader AND an empowered Sweetheart (second encounter, she came back stronger). They fight together. Party grows to three.

### Beat 6 — Mage
A tower is nearby. Fighter drags Mage out of it. Mage complains the entire time. He comes anyway. The original four are reunited.

### Beat 7 — The Shattering & Henry Stickmin
Some time after Mage joins, the Shattering event hits. Henry Stickmin falls face-first out of the sky and lands next to the party. He joins. No further explanation is given or needed.

### Act 1 End
The original party is back plus Henry. The scope of the shattering becomes clear. The real journey starts.

## 4. THE COLOR SYSTEM

### Overview
Every special attack in the game belongs to one of five colors. Colors serve as both an elemental type system AND a mechanical identity. Characters have two colors — a primary and a secondary. Primary color skills are learned naturally and deal boosted damage/effects. Secondary color gives access to some skills with a smaller or no buff. Enemies have weaknesses and resistances tied to the color system.

### The Five Colors

| Color | Element | Mechanical Identity | Skill flavor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Red | Fire | Aggression, burn DoT, high damage, fast skills, low cost | Offensive, impulsive, direct |
| Blue | Water / Ice | Freeze, slow, debuffs, turn manipulation, resource drain, control | Calculated, reactive, disruptive |
| Green | Earth / Nature | Regen, stat buffs, brute force hits, high HP scaling | Endurance, growth, overwhelming force |
| White | Holy | Healing, shields, buffs, status cleanse, resurrection | Order, protection, unity |
| Black | Unholy | Life drain, DEF-piercing damage, status infliction, sacrifice HP for power | Ambition, corruption, self-advancement |

### Dual Color Rules
- Every character has exactly two colors
- Primary color: character learns these skills by default, gets a damage/effect buff
- Secondary color: partial skill access, smaller or no buff
- Color identity shapes build direction and party composition decisions

### Color Interaction Chart

**The Elemental Triangle (RGB)**
| Attacker | Defender | Modifier |
|---|---|---|
| Red | Green | 2x damage |
| Green | Red | 0.5x damage |
| Green | Blue | 2x damage |
| Blue | Green | 0.5x damage |
| Blue | Red | 2x damage |
| Red | Blue | 0.5x damage |

**Holy vs Unholy (BW)**
| Attacker | Defender | Modifier |
|---|---|---|
| White | Black | 2x damage |
| Black | White | 2x damage |

No resistance in either direction — White and Black purely amplify damage against each other. Neither reduces incoming damage from the other.

**Cross-System**
- RGB colors have no bonus or penalty against White or Black
- White and Black have no interaction with the elemental triangle
- The two systems coexist independently

### Enemy Color Interactions
- Enemies have one or more color weaknesses and resistances based on this chart
- Bringing the wrong color into a fight = 0.5x damage output
- Bringing the right color = 2x damage output
- Party color diversity is rewarded, mono-color teams are punished in the wrong matchup

### Realm & Color Interactions
- Certain realms amplify or suppress specific colors
- Heaven: TBD interaction with White
- Hell: TBD interaction with Black
- RGB Realm: TBD interaction with Red, Green, Blue
- BW Realm: TBD interaction with Black, White
- Details TBD per realm as they are developed

- Notes: Fighter is the story's protagonist — not a player self-insert. He has his own personality, motivations, and arc. The player watches him, not projects onto him. The only original party member not named after their class — played as a joke about his intellect. He starts the whole thing by walking out of retirement out of sheer boredom.

#### Cleric

- Personality: Warm, fully committed, emotionally invested in everyone. Was actively searching for Thief when the party reunited. In love with Fighter who cannot or will not see it.

#### Mage
- Class: Mage. NOT a Wizard. Do not call him a Wizard.
- Personality: Fighter's best friend. The strongest in the realm — not just the room, the REALM — and his achievements regrettably reinforce this. Holds back constantly, by choice. Currently estimated at 8-15% actual capacity.
- Colors: TBD (likely Blue/Red — control and chaos)
- Resource Pool: Mana (MP)
- Role: Magic/elemental damage dealer. The actual ceiling of the party's power, deliberately suppressed.
- Battle Theme: "Move B*tch Get Out The Way" / "X Gon Give It To Ya" energy — arrives and owns the room, correct about everything, completely indifferent to how that makes everyone feel
- Holding Back: Mage holds back by choice. The party rags on him for it constantly. He does not change his behavior. "I'm at 12%." "Would you like me to demonstrate 13%?"
- The Wizard Issue: Dissolved the Wizard Ward. Founded the Mage Methodology in its place. Ran it for exactly one year. Left because administration kept getting in the way of learning spells. The Mage Methodology still exists and thrives. He has never returned. A building was named after him. He has not visited the building. The Wizard Ward also still technically exists somewhere. Nobody has told Mage. Nobody is going to tell Mage.
- Zone of Truth: Counterspells Zone of Truth on principle, every time, before the cast completes. Never looks up from his book.
- Party Dynamics:
  - Fighter: knows Mage is probably stronger, doesn't care, they're bros, moving on
  - Thief: highest threat assessment in the log, never mentioned
  - Cleric: quietly grateful, slightly terrified
  - Sorcerer: argues chaos magic is superior, Mage lets her be wrong, this makes her furious
  - Paladin: Zone of Truth attempt — counterspelled before cast completes, every time, on principle
- Notes: Fighter and Mage are bros. Bros bros bros. Previous friendly bout left Fighter simultaneously Poisoned, Paralyzed, Frightened, Cursed, Cold, and Burned. Fighter was grinning the entire time.

## 6. COMBAT SYSTEM

### Overview
Turn-based combat inspired by Persona 5. Encounters are fast, readable, and reward smart play without being slow. Both sides occupy a 3x3 grid — positioning is a core mechanic that affects damage dealt, damage taken, and target access.

### Party Structure
- **3 Active** characters on the field occupying positions on the player 3x3 grid
- **3 Reserve** characters on the bench (off grid)
- Reserve characters can act and swap freely — swapping does not cost a turn
- 3 active fills the 3x3 grid cleanly with meaningful positioning choices and no empty slots
- Characters choose their position within the grid — not all 9 slots need to be filled but 3 active characters place naturally without gaps looking bad

### Turn Order
- Determined by Speed stat (derived — TBD exact formula)
- Status effects and skills can modify turn order

### Action Menu (Per Turn)
- Attack — Basic physical attack
- Skill — Uses character's resource pool, belongs to a color
- Item — Use consumable from inventory
- Swap — Bring in a reserve character (free action)
- Move — Reposition on the grid (unlimited times per turn, free)
- Defend — Reduce incoming damage this turn
- Flee — Attempt escape (LCK influences success rate)

---

### The 3x3 Combat Grid

Both the player party and enemies occupy their own 3x3 grid. The two grids face each other. Position on the grid affects damage output, damage received, and melee access.

```
PLAYER GRID                    ENEMY GRID
+--------+--------+--------+  +--------+--------+--------+
| [P1]   | [P2]   | [P3]   |  | [E1]   | [E2]   | [E3]   |
| Back   | Back   | Back   |  | Back   | Back   | Back   |
+--------+--------+--------+  +--------+--------+--------+
| [P4]   | [P5]   | [P6]   |  | [E4]   | [E5]   | [E6]   |
| Middle | Middle | Middle |  | Middle | Middle | Middle |
+--------+--------+--------+  +--------+--------+--------+
| [P7]   | [P8]   | [P9]   |  | [E7]   | [E8]   | [E9]   |
| Front  | Front  | Front  |  | Front  | Front  | Front  |
+--------+--------+--------+  +--------+--------+--------+
              <--- Facing Enemy --->
```

**Row Positioning Modifiers**
| Row | Damage Dealt | Damage Taken | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Front | +25% | +25% | High risk, high reward |
| Middle | Normal | Normal | Balanced position |
| Back | -25% | -25% | Safe but reduced output |

Same rules apply to enemies — front row enemies hit harder and take more damage.

---

### Melee Targeting Rules

```
Example: Player in Back-Left wants to melee Enemy in Back-Right

PLAYER GRID         ENEMY GRID
[ ]  [ ]  [ ]      [TARGET] [ ]  [ ]   <- Back row
[ ]  [ ]  [ ]      [BLOCKER][ ]  [ ]   <- Middle row  
[P]  [ ]  [ ]      [ ]      [ ]  [ ]   <- Front row

BLOCKER occupies the same column between P and TARGET.
Melee bonus is denied. Attack still lands but at normal damage.
Remove BLOCKER first to unlock the bonus.
```

**Melee Rules**
- Can target ANY enemy on the grid — no hard range restriction
- Deals front row bonus damage (+25%) when attacking enemies in the closest row
- If an enemy occupies the same column between attacker and target — melee bonus is blocked
- Attack still connects at normal damage, bonus is just denied
- Clearing front column enemies opens up melee bonuses on back rows
- Movement is free and unlimited — reposition before attacking to find open columns

---

### Ranged & Magic Targeting Rules
- Ranged (bows, guns) and Magic attacks can hit any grid position freely
- No column blocking mechanic — shots and spells go over or around obstacles
- No front row proximity bonus — distance is irrelevant for these attack types
- Row modifiers still apply to the TARGET (front row enemy still takes +25%)

---

### Attack Categories
Every attack belongs to one of three categories:
- **Melee** — physical close combat, swords, fists, blades
- **Ranged** — bows, guns, thrown weapons (non-magic)
- **Magic** — spells, colored skills, elemental attacks

Attack category matters for Ward interactions (see below).

---

### Wards

Magic enemies can cast wards that block an entire attack category for the whole party.

**Standard Enemy Wards**
- Block one category: Melee, Ranged, or Magic
- Affects the entire player party — not just one character
- Expire naturally after a set number of turns
- Can be broken early by ward-breaker skills
- Forces party to adapt and use unblocked attack types

**Boss Ward Cycle**
Bosses don't use a single ward — they cycle through all three in a random order on a fixed timer.

```
BOSS WARD CYCLE EXAMPLE:

Turn 1-4:   [MAGIC BLOCKED]   [MELEE: OPEN]   [RANGED: OPEN]
Turn 5-8:   [RANGED BLOCKED]  [MELEE: OPEN]   [MAGIC: OPEN]
Turn 9-12:  [MELEE BLOCKED]   [RANGED: OPEN]  [MAGIC: OPEN]
Turn 13+:   Cycle repeats in new random order
```

- Order is **random** each fight — never the same sequence
- Timing is **fixed per boss tier**:

| Boss Tier | Turns Per Ward | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Early Game | 4-5 turns | Forgiving, teaches the mechanic |
| Mid Game | 3-4 turns | Tighter windows, more pressure |
| Late Game | 2-3 turns | Fast rotation, constant adaptation |
| Endgame / Superboss | TBD | Probably brutal |

- Ward-breaker skills can shatter active wards early
- Players MUST cover all three attack categories against bosses
- Mono-category parties will always have a window where they're shut down

### Resource Pools
Each character has a uniquely named resource pool reflecting their identity:
- Fighter: Stamina (SP)
- Thief: Power (PWR)
- Cleric: Mana (MP)
- Mage: Mana (MP)
- Warrior: Stamina (STM)
- Henry Stickmin: NRG Drink (NRG)
- All others: TBD per character identity

### Damage Types
- Physical — scales with ATK vs DEF, no color, belongs to Melee or Ranged category
- Magic/Elemental — scales with MAG vs MDEF, carries color type, belongs to Magic category
- Elemental affinity and color access granted by character identity and gear
- Enemy weaknesses and resistances apply to color types

### Damage Calculation Formula

**Physical Hit:**
```
Base        = ATK - DEF
            = 0 if ATK ≤ DEF (fully blocked)
After Mods  = Base × Color Modifier × Crit Modifier
Final       = After Mods + (After Mods × Row Bonus)
```

**Magic Hit:**
```
Base        = MAG - MDEF
            = 0 if MAG ≤ MDEF (fully blocked)
After Mods  = Base × Color Modifier × Crit Modifier
Final       = After Mods + (After Mods × Row Bonus)
```

**DEF is strictly physical only. MDEF is strictly magic only. No crossover.**

**Modifier Values:**
| Modifier | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Color Weakness | ×2.0 | Attacker's color beats defender's |
| Color Resistance | ×0.5 | Defender resists attacker's color |
| Color Neutral | ×1.0 | No interaction |
| Critical Hit | ×2.0 | Doubles final damage |
| Front Row | +25% additive | Applied after crit/color |
| Middle Row | +0% | No modifier |
| Back Row | -25% additive | Applied after crit/color |

**Stacking example — front row + weakness + crit:**
`Base × 2.0 (weak) × 2.0 (crit) = 4× base, then +25% additive = 5× effective`

**Blocked attack:** If ATK - DEF ≤ 0, damage = 0. Defense is a real wall, not a soft reduction.

---

### Enemy HP Scaling
Enemy HP scales dynamically based on average player damage at that level rather than fixed values. This ensures the ratio of hits-to-kill stays consistent regardless of level.

**Formula:** `Enemy Total HP = Average Player Damage at Level × HP Ratio`

| Enemy Type | HP Ratio | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Common (Early) | ×5 | 3-6 hits to kill |
| Elite (Mid) | ×12 | 8-15 hits to kill |
| Heavy (Late) | ×20 | Tanky, built tough |
| Early Boss | ×35 | 2 health bars |
| Mid Boss | ×60 | 3 health bars |
| Late Boss (Echo Tau) | ×120 | 4 health bars |

**Why ratio scaling:** Numbers will reach hundreds of thousands at high levels and that is fine. Disgaea logic applies — the ratio staying consistent is what matters, not the raw number.

---

### Boss Health Bars & Phase Transitions
Bosses have multiple health bars. Each bar break is a phase transition — a natural pause where the boss reacts, shifts behavior, and escalates.

| Boss Tier | Health Bars |
|---|---|
| Early (Sweetheart tier) | 2 bars |
| Mid (Realm Warden tier) | 3 bars |
| Late (Echo Tau tier) | 4 bars |
| Endgame / New Tabuu | TBD — probably a lot |

**Phase transition events (per boss, handcrafted individually):**
- New random ward cycle begins
- New attack patterns unlock
- Boss dialogue / story reaction
- Visual change — sprite, aura, color shift
- Desperation mechanics on final bar (shorter ward timers, higher damage, unique moves)

Each boss's phases are designed individually to feel handcrafted rather than templated. The framework provides the structure, the personality fills it in.

### Node Data (Reusable Code Structure)
Every node in the game uses the same `NodeData` resource. Change the values, completely different node. Same code runs all of them.

```gdscript
class_name NodeData
extends Resource

@export var node_name: String = ""
@export var node_type: String = ""       # combat, town, dungeon, story, boss, character
@export var unlock_cost: int = 0         # currency cost to enter
@export var unlock_requirement: String = "" # what node must be cleared first
@export var enemy_pool: Array = []       # what enemies can appear
@export var boss_id: String = ""         # which boss guards this node
@export var reward_xp: int = 0
@export var reward_gold: int = 0
@export var is_completed: bool = false
```

## 13. MONSTERPEDIA (Working Title)

### Overview
An in-game bestiary that tracks every enemy the player has encountered. Each entry is written from the perspective of one party member — chosen for maximum personality fit with that specific enemy. One voice per monster, curated, characterful.

### Unlock System
- **Seen** — entry unlocks on first encounter. Shows silhouette only, name, and "???" for stats.
- **Defeated** — full entry unlocks after first defeat. Shows stats, weaknesses, resistances, drop table.
- **Complete** — all data filled in after multiple encounters.

### Entry Structure
Each monster entry contains:
- **Name**
- **Description** — brief flavor text about the enemy
- **Character Note** — one line from a specific party member's perspective
- **Stats** — HP ratio, DEF, MDEF, ATK
- **Color Weaknesses** — what colors deal 2x damage
- **Color Resistances** — what colors deal 0.5x damage
- **Drop Table** — what loot they can drop and at what rarity
- **Realm** — which realm they come from

### Shiny Encounters

A small chance on any enemy encounter to spawn a shiny version — visually distinct, significantly stronger, but much more rewarding.

**Shiny Stats (applied at runtime via code — no separate data files needed):**
- HP: ×2
- ATK: ×2
- DEF/MDEF: ×2
- Gold dropped: ×3
- XP rewarded: ×3
- Drop rolls: ×2 (rolls the drop table twice)

**Encounter Rate:**
- Base chance: 2% per encounter
- LCK influences rate — higher party average LCK = higher shiny chance
- Formula: `shiny_chance = 0.02 + (party_avg_lck × 0.001)`
- Soft cap TBD — never guaranteed but meaningfully farmable with LCK builds

**Visual Identity (must be immediately recognizable):**
- Bright glowing outline or aura
- Color shifted from normal version
- Particle effect sparkling around them
- Distinct sound cue on spawn
- Player should know it's a shiny before reading any stats

**Technical Implementation:**
- One enemy data file per enemy type — normal version only
- On battle load, roll `randf() < shiny_chance`
- If shiny — load normal data, call `apply_shiny()`, swap visual
- `apply_shiny()` handles all multipliers universally
- Every enemy gets shiny support automatically — no manual setup per enemy

**Monsterpedia:** Shiny versions have their own separate entry — discovered and completed independently from the normal version.

## 14. UNLEASH SYSTEM

### Overview
A desperation/rage bar that fills as characters take damage. The lower the HP the faster it fills. When full the player can trigger a character's unique Unleash move — a powerful, personality-defining attack that's been building the whole fight.

### The Bar
- **Name:** Unleash
- **Capacity:** 200 points (adjustable)
- **Fill Rate:** 1 point minimum per hit, up to 50 points maximum per hit
- **Scaling:** The lower the character's HP the more points gained per hit
- **Target fill time:** 5-7 rounds of taking damage
- **Trigger:** Manual — player selects Unleash as an action when bar is full
- **UI:** Unleash button lights up / becomes available when bar reaches 200

### Point Gain Formula
```gdscript
func get_unleash_points(damage_taken: int, current_hp: int, max_hp: int) -> int:
    var hp_percent = float(current_hp) / float(max_hp)
    var base_points = damage_taken * 0.5
    var desperation_bonus = (1.0 - hp_percent) * 50
    return int(clamp(base_points + desperation_bonus, 1, 50))
```

- **Full HP** — slow fill, base points only
- **50% HP** — desperation bonus kicks in noticeably
- **25% HP** — filling fast, getting dangerous
- **Near death** — almost maxing per hit

### Design Intent
Players who take risks and absorb damage fill the bar faster. Defending constantly is safe but delays access to Unleash. Risk and reward baked into the core loop.

---

### Confirmed Unleash Moves

#### Fighter — Reckoning
Select one primary target. That target receives 200% of Fighter's normal attack damage, hit 10 times. All other enemies receive 90% of each hit as collateral damage. Fighter doesn't try to clear the field — he makes a point about one specific enemy and everyone else just happens to be nearby.

#### Thief — Overclock
Multi-hit single target attack. Steals 3 items from the target. Efficient as always — why waste a trip.

#### Cleric — Last Rites
Phase 1: Cleric nukes the entire field with unholy magic — ALL allies are reduced to 1 HP.
Phase 2: Immediately full heals all allies.
The risk window between Phase 1 and Phase 2 is intentional. She trusts herself completely. If something interrupts the heal, that's a very bad day. Battle cleric energy at its absolute peak.

#### Mage — "I THINK THE FUCK NOT"
Nukes all enemies using the full might of arcana. No formal move name. No charge up. No dramatic pose. He looks up from his book for exactly one second, raises his staff, says the words, everything dies, returns to reading. The damage output is not discussed in polite company.

## PLANNED FUTURE MECHANIC — Attack Type Triangle (RPS System)

*To be implemented after core battle system is stable.*

### Overview
A Rock Paper Scissors layer on top of the existing color weakness/resistance system. Inspired by Monster Hunter Stories. Each enemy has a current stance — hitting them with the right attack type deals bonus damage on top of color modifiers.

### The Triangle
**Power → Technical → Speed → Power**

- **Power** — heavy hits, slow, beats Technical
- **Speed** — fast multi-hits, beats Power
- **Technical** — precise, status focused, beats Speed

### How It Interacts With Existing Systems
A hit gets evaluated in order:
1. Base damage (ATK/MAG vs DEF/MDEF)
2. Color modifier (weakness/resistance)
3. Attack type modifier (RPS matchup)
4. Row bonus
5. Crit

Best case scenario — front row, color weakness, correct attack type, crit — results in massive damage. High skill expression reward.

### Enemy Stance
- Enemies have a current stance that cycles or reacts to player actions
- Wrong type = reduced damage or enemy counter
- Correct type = bonus damage multiplier (TBD)
- Stance is visible to the player — readable from enemy behavior or UI indicator

### Implementation Note
The damage formula already supports adding another multiplier step. No structural changes needed — just add the stance check alongside the color check when ready.
